# Is there a contribution beyond continued citation?

Decision on 2026-11-04: **narrow and test**. Stop the paper path framed only as
"papers that do not replicate continue to be cited." Continue a bounded I4R
measurement/feasibility study. A larger dataset or different field alone is not
the claimed novelty.

## Closest prior work

| Work | What it already establishes or studies | Consequence here |
|---|---|---|
| [Serra-Garcia and Gneezy (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abd1705) | Nonreplicable papers received more citations, with little change in the gap after replication; 12% of examined post-replication citations acknowledged failure. | Neither continued citation nor acknowledgment rates are new outcomes. |
| [Schafmeister (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1177/09567976211005767) | Uses 95 RP:P originals and nearby journal articles to study citation changes; finds little evidence of adjustment. | Matching and replication-success comparisons have precedents. |
| [von Hippel (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1177/17456916211072525) | Examines 98 psychology originals; estimated failure-associated changes relative to successful replications were small and uncertain; co-citation was rare. | Favorable versus unfavorable news and co-citation are established comparisons. |
| [Clark, Connor and Isch (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2304862120) | Reports a modeled citation decline following failed replications among 228 psychology papers. | Findings are mixed across designs. Continued citation can coexist with a penalty. The local replication_citations project already reproduces this analysis. |
| [Coupé and Reed (2022 working paper)](https://repec.canterbury.ac.nz/cbt/econwp/2216.pdf) | Studies 204 replicated economics papers using matched citation histories; finds no clear negative-replication penalty. | Economics and matched counterfactual citation paths are not new territory. |
| [Ankel-Peters, Fiala and Neubauer (online 2024; issue 2025)](https://doi.org/10.1111/ecin.13222) | Studies 56 AER comments from 2010–2020, substantive concerns, replies, citation trends and acknowledgment. | Distinguishing consequential criticism and reading author replies are insufficient novelty by themselves. Their main trend analysis is descriptive, not a randomized or econometric causal estimate. |
| [Hardwicke et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1177/25152459211040837) | Four strongly contradictory replication cases, favorable/unfavorable citation contexts, acknowledgment and explicit defenses. | Simply adding context coding is also not new. This is the closest measurement precedent. |
| [I4R team, Nature (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10251-x) | Reports computational reproducibility and robustness assessments of 110 economics/political-science articles. | Replication rates and robustness heterogeneity already have a direct I4R study. The proposed outcome is downstream response, not another estimate of those rates. |

Sources were checked through publisher, author, or institutional copies on
2026-11-04. Targeted searches included replication/citation effects, I4R citation
studies, and citation-context work. This is a focused novelty review, not a
systematic review proving that no related study exists. The 110-article overview
and its discussion-paper versions must not be counted as separate cohorts.

## What could survive that comparison

The candidate contribution is a claim-linked response record across heterogeneous
assessments: identify precisely what changed, whether the original conclusion
was strengthened, weakened, or only qualified, and whether subsequent research
uses that specific claim. An unaffected methods citation is different from
unqualified reliance on a challenged estimate; citing a report is different from
adopting its correction. Separate documented author/journal responses from later
researchers' citation behavior.

The scientific payoff would be learning where correction uptake breaks down and
whether demonstrable errors, disputed robustness choices, and new-data results
are followed by different kinds of use. These are observational differences;
severity, selection into assessment, visibility, field, and time since publication
can explain them. The study cannot infer awareness from silence or establish that
all continued use is irrational.

## Current reasons not to scale yet

The frozen registry has unresolved assessments and incomplete event timing for
the broad comparison groups. The curated material-error subset was selected and
reviewed for an earlier question. Its three inherited matched cases do not establish
a new population-level effect. Recent I4R assessments have little completed
follow-up. Claim-context access and coding reliability have not been measured.

The first discriminating test is the fixed, probability-sampled context queue in
`data/feasibility/`. If claim-specific use cannot be separated reliably from
background, method, and unaffected-result citations, do not replace missing
measurement with a citation-count paper under a new title. Preserve the registry
as a research resource and reconsider the paper contribution.
